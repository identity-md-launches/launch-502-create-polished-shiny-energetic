02
Good vs. bad examples

Make the answer checkable: what counts, by when, and according to whom?

Bad question
“Will there be a big earthquake tomorrow?”

How big? Where? Tomorrow in which timezone? Two people could read this differently.

Good question
“Will USGS record an earthquake of magnitude 5.0 or higher anywhere in the world on 1 December 2026, UTC?”

The USGS earthquake catalog is the source. Check the event’s occurrence time, from 00:00 up to the next midnight UTC.

YES — at least one qualifying event.
NO — the complete record shows none.
VOID — the record is unavailable or cannot establish the answer.

Check the times too. STOP closes betting. Ask Oracle comes after the evidence is available—it is not a payout deadline. Anyone can publish; read the exact question and rules before betting.

When does the Oracle answer?

We request a result only at or after Ask Oracle, and only if money matched. Each request publishes its panel size and required agreement. Open the market’s Oracle details to see the votes.

Ask Oracle is the earliest request time, not a payout deadline. Delays or disagreement can leave matched funds locked until the market is finalized.

Who can finalize or correct a result?

For now, an operator reviews the IMD Oracle result before settlement; votes and the market-bound signed proof are checked automatically.

No operator can emergency-VOID matched bets before Ask Oracle. At or after Ask, an authorized operator can VOID an unresolved matched market with a public reason, even if no Oracle request was made or no answer arrived. Users can then claim their matched stakes; earned fees stay paid. Ask does not trigger an automatic refund or guarantee recovery by a deadline.

Authorized recovery roles can also replace a failed request or finalize an unresolved market as YES or NO from Ask, with a public reason. A finalized result cannot be changed. Automatic finalization is switchable; operator review is the current default.
